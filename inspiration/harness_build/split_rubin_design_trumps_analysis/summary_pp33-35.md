# Rubin, "For Objective Causal Inference, Design Trumps Analysis" — pp. 33-35

## Content
These pages are entirely the **References** section (no argument or example). The
paper ends here; the author affiliation is Department of Statistics, Harvard
University (rubin@stat.harvard.edu).

## What the bibliography reveals about the argument
The reference list situates the "design trumps analysis" thesis in a lineage worth
noting for the design-before-analysis theme:

- **Cochran's observational-studies program**: Cochran (1965) "The planning of
  observational studies," Cochran (1968) on subclassification for bias removal,
  Cochran (1983) *Planning and Analysis of Observational Studies* — Rubin's design
  emphasis descends directly from Cochran.
- **Experimental-design canon**: Fisher (1925, 1935), Cox (1958) *The Planning of
  Experiments*, Kempthorne (1952), Cochran & Cox (1950) — the randomized-experiment
  ideal that observational design tries to reconstruct.
- **Rubin Causal Model / potential outcomes**: Neyman (1923), Rubin (1974, 1978,
  1990a, 2005), Holland (1986).
- **Propensity scores**: Rosenbaum & Rubin (1983) "central role of the propensity
  score," Rosenbaum & Rubin (1985), Rubin (1997, 2002), Rubin & Thomas (1992, 2000),
  Rubin (2006) *Matched Sampling for Causal Effects*.
- **Principal stratification / IV**: Angrist, Imbens & Rubin (1996), Frangakis &
  Rubin (2002), Imbens & Rubin (1997), Jin & Rubin (2008).
- **The companion "outcome-free design" papers** that most directly echo this
  paper's thesis: Rubin (2007) "The design versus the analysis of observational
  studies for causal effects," Rubin (2008), and Langenskold & Rubin (2008)
  "Outcome-free design of observational studies."
- **Cardia-cancer follow-up**: Rubin, Wang, Yin & Zell (2008) — the Bayesian
  principal-stratification analysis of the Swedish hospital-type data used as the
  running example.

## DESIGN-BEFORE-ANALYSIS
No new argument, but the citations reinforce the thesis: the phrase "outcome-free
design" (Langenskold & Rubin 2008) is the bibliographic banner for the principle
that design must precede any look at outcomes; Rubin (2007, Stat. Med.) is the
explicit "design versus analysis" companion.
