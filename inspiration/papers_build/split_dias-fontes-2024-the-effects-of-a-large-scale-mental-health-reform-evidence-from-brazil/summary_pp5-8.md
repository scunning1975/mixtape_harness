# Dias & Fontes (2024), Mental Health Reform / Brazil — pp. 5-8

## What these pages argue
Completes institutional background, gives the theory of change, and lays out ALL the data sources. Emphasizes practical weakness of CAPS (under-resourced, part-time doctors, no beds, not 24h — unlike US community mental health centers/CMHCs; only CAPS III resembles CMHCs and few exist). Patient-mix mismatch: CAPS mostly treat severe cases (28% schizophrenia) yet may be ill-equipped for them. Notes that psychiatric-bed decline in Brazil predates and runs independent of CAPS — so bed reduction is NOT cleanly a CAPS first stage. Theory of change (Figure 2): CAPS construction -> shifts locus of care & outpatient supply -> new market equilibrium (inpatient vs outpatient production) -> effects on public spending, population mental health, and violent crime.

### BITE / FIRST STAGE
This chunk is the CORE of a bite measure — it defines every treatment-volume dataset:
- **Professional density (bite input):** From CNES (National Registry of Health Establishments). For each municipality, count mental-health providers = psychiatrists + psychologists + occupational therapists + social workers (these = ~87% of CAPS staff). Also number of hospital beds, selecting psychiatric beds.
- **Outpatient treatment VOLUME:** SIA (National System of Information on Ambulatory Care) — all publicly funded outpatient visits. From 2008 on, can identify provider type; they take the overall number of outpatient services by each mental-health provider type. This is the direct outpatient-visits bite.
- **Drug/pharmaceutical bite:** SIA "outpatient pharmaceutical assistance" — they focus on ANTIPSYCHOTIC medications distributed per municipality.
- **Untreated / inpatient margin (substitution):** SIH (Hospital Information System) — public-sector hospital admissions by patient's municipality of residence and by ICD-10 cause; used to identify mental-illness hospitalizations (grouped, Appendix Table A3). Expected to FALL = deinstitutionalization/substitution. Psychiatric BEDS (from CNES) also on the untreated margin.
- **Take-up logic:** After CAPS opens, patients switch from (public-clinic outpatient / inpatient / no treatment) toward CAPS outpatient care; other units increase referrals. Focus = substitution of inpatient with outpatient.
- **Spending bite:** federal spending on admissions (SIH) + municipal health spending (National Treasury).

### DiD DESIGN
- Reaffirmed: staggered CAPS rollout, municipality-level, de Chaisemartin-D'Haultfoeuille estimator.
- Outcome channels tied to Figure 2: care supply, market equilibrium (in/outpatient), public spending, population mental health (mortality), violent crime (homicides).

### DATA DETAILS
All from Brazilian Ministry of Health / DATASUS unless noted:
- **CNES** (DATASUS 1979-2024a): facilities, professionals, beds.
- **SIA** (1979-2024d): outpatient visits (provider type from 2008) + antipsychotic drugs dispensed.
- **SIH** (1979-2024e): hospital admissions by residence & ICD-10 cause; also federal admission spending.
- **SIM** (mortality; introduced next chunk).
- **National Treasury** (IPEA 2023b): municipal health spending.
- Unit = municipality (minimum comparable areas via IPEA); policy implemented at municipality level.
