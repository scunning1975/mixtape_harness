# Dias & Fontes (2024) — pp. 285-288 (Spillovers, Conclusion, References begin)

These pages close the robustness section (spillover/displacement test), deliver the Conclusion, and begin the reference list. The substantive economics ends here; the paper's thesis is restated cleanly.

## BITE / FIRST STAGE
The Conclusion restates the first stage qualitatively (no new numbers on this chunk):
- The reform "achieved its primary goal of **increasing the supply of outpatient mental care and shifting care away from inpatient to outpatient settings**."
- Crucially, "the effects are **concentrated on long-stay admissions and patients with schizophrenia**" — i.e., the deinstitutionalization bite hit exactly the crime-prone, hard-to-treat margin. This is the treatment-intensity link to the homicide outcome.
- Coverage caveat for external validity: **less than 3% of Brazilian municipalities** have 24-hour mental-health centers with outpatient beds and crisis intervention — the intensive-care infrastructure that could prevent violence is largely absent.

## MAIN RESULTS / MECHANISM
**Spillover test (Sec V.C):** Online App Fig A10 compares treated vs. non-treated means over event time. Changes are concentrated in the treatment group; control areas never break trend in the opposite direction => no displacement/spillover contamination of the homicide result.

**Conclusion — four findings:** (1) reform increased outpatient supply and shifted inpatient→outpatient; (2) substitution concentrated on long-stay + schizophrenia, an illness more severe than most CAPS can treat; (3) despite community care being cheaper per patient, **public spending rose** with the reform (cost-ineffective); (4) **persistent increase in homicide rates**, attributed to lost incapacitation of the crime-prone mentally ill (Penrose). Claims to be the first causal evaluation of a large-scale mental-health reform. Policy lesson: an inadequate substitute for severe illness (schizophrenia) harms patients and the community; intensive community-care models needed. No suicide/deaths-of-despair effect reaffirmed implicitly (homicide is the live outcome).
