# Miller, Johnson & Wherry (2021), pp. 25-28 — Summary

## What these pages contain
These are the opening pages of the **References** section (bibliography). No new argument, equation, or result is presented here; the pages are the alphabetized citation list running roughly A–M (Allen through Musumeci). They document the literature the paper builds on and rebuts.

## Key clusters of citations (what they signal about the paper's scaffolding)
- **Prior Medicaid-mortality evidence** the paper engages with: Sommers, Long & Baicker (Massachusetts reform, mortality); Goodman-Bacon (2018, Medicaid implementation and mortality, *JPE*); Black, Hollingsworth, Nunes & Simon (2019, power analysis — the "is the ACA underpowered to detect mortality?" critique); Borgschulte & Vogler (2020, "Did the ACA Medicaid Expansion Save Lives?"); Chen (2019); Khatana et al. (cardiovascular mortality).
- **Oregon Health Insurance Experiment**: Baicker et al. (2013, *NEJM*), Finkelstein et al. (2012, *QJE*) — the RCT benchmark.
- **DiD methodology**: Goodman-Bacon (2019) DiD with variation in timing; bacondecomp module; Conley (1999) spatial standard errors; Imbens & Angrist (1994) LATE.
- **Measurement / linkage validation** (crucial for their data design): Boudreaux et al. (2013, 2015) on measurement error in Medicaid reporting in the ACS via record linkage; Bond et al. (2014) bias from linkable-only records; Mulrow et al. (2011) Census PVS validation; Fernandez et al. (2015).
- **ACA coverage/access effects**: Miller & Wherry (2017, 2019); Wherry & Miller (2016); Courtemanche et al.; Frean, Gruber & Sommers (2017); Kaestner-related childhood Medicaid work (Brown, Kowalski & Lurie; Goodman-Bacon 2016; Boudreaux, Golberstein & McAlpine).
- **Economic-shock confounders** they must rule out: Autor, Dorn & Hanson (China Syndrome; falling marriage-market value); Alpert, Evans, Lieber & Powell (opioid crisis origins) — foreshadows opioid/trade robustness controls.
