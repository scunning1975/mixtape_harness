# Summary: Cengiz, Dube, Lindner & Zipperer (2019), pp. 1437–1440

## What these pages argue
These pages complete the sector table, present the incumbent-vs-new-entrant analysis (Section III.A.3), and open the wage-spillovers section (III.B). The key new result: partitioning workers into those employed before the increase (incumbents) and new entrants shows the minimum wage clearly binds for *both* groups, with excess jobs matching missing jobs in each — no net job loss. Incumbents show no job destruction and receive most of the wage "ripple" (spillover) effect, further evidence against labor-labor substitution. This motivates the transition to studying wage spillovers directly.

## Key concepts / equations
- **Incumbents** = employed one year before the MW increase; **new entrants** = not employed a year prior. Partition exploits the CPS interviewing each respondent twice, exactly one year apart (outgoing rotation group in month 4).
- Same event-study design (equation (1)); Figure IV plots per-dollar-bin employment changes plus a **running sum** of employment changes across bins.
- Incumbent-only studies (Currie-Fallick 1996; Abowd et al. 2000; Clemens-Wither 2019) give only a partial picture because new entrants are also affected.

## Main results / numbers
- **Sector bite / employment** (Table III): restaurants Δb −10.1%, employment insignificant; tradeable/manufacturing negative but imprecise (pp.1438).
- **Incumbents:** Δa = 1.3%, Δb = −1.2%; affected wage +9.5% (s.e. 2.0%); % affected employment 0.009 (s.e. 0.068).
- **New entrants:** Δa = 0.6%, Δb = −0.5%; affected wage +1.9% (s.e. 1.3%); % affected employment 0.008 (s.e. 0.034).
- Net employment change ≈ 0 for both groups; little change in upper-tail employment (running sums flat).
- The wage ripple/spillover comes **mainly from incumbent workers** (9.5% vs 1.9%).

### DATA DETAILS
- **Table III (sectors):** 118 events; 554,931 observations (quarter-bin cells) per column; restricted to **1992–2016**; worker counts per sector as listed p.1437.
- **Incumbent/new-entrant partition:** covers **1980–2016**, **137 eligible MW-raising events**; time window restricted to **one year** around the increase (not the 5-year baseline).
- **CPS rotation mechanics:** respondents interviewed 4 months, rotated out 8 months, rotated back 4 months; wage questions asked in month 4 (outgoing rotation group). Workers matched across rotation groups per Online Appendix E.
- Figure IV built from 138 state-level MW changes, 1979–2016 (baseline design); wage bins in $ relative to new MW, spanning −4 to 17+.
- SEs clustered by state throughout.
