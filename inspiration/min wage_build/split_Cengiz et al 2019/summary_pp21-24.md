# Summary: Cengiz, Dube, Lindner & Zipperer (2019), pp. 1425–1428

## What these pages argue
These pages report the paper's headline employment and wage results from the "bunching" event-study estimator. The core finding: minimum wage increases produce a sharp drop in jobs paying *below* the new minimum (the "missing jobs," Δb) that is almost exactly offset by a rise in jobs paying *at or just above* it (the "excess jobs," Δa). Net employment among affected workers is essentially unchanged, while wages rise. The design uses excess/missing job counts to recover both an employment elasticity and a wage effect.

## Key concepts / equations
- **Δb** = missing jobs, counted from $4 below the new minimum wage up to the new minimum.
- **Δa** = excess jobs, from the new minimum wage up to $5 above it.
- **Employment change = Δa + Δb**; dividing by pretreatment jobs-below (b̄₋₁) gives % affected employment; dividing by %ΔMW gives the minimum-wage employment elasticity.
- **Equation (2)** used to back out the average-wage change and the own-wage (labor-demand) employment elasticity.
- Estimates normalized to event date τ = −1 (year before treatment); event time runs τ = −3 to +4.

## Main results / numbers
- % affected employment: **2.8%** (s.e. 2.9%), insignificant.
- Employment elasticity w.r.t. MW: **0.024** (s.e. 0.025); 95% CI rules out Meer-West (2016) baseline of −0.074.
- Teen-comparable adjustment: multiply by 5.02 (=0.432/0.086), giving affected-share-adjusted 95% CI of **[−0.13, 0.37]**, ruling out most of the −0.1 to −0.3 range.
- Effect on average wages: **6.8%** (s.e. 1.0%), significant.
- Own-wage employment elasticity: **0.411** (s.e. 0.430); rules out anything below −0.450.
- Robustness Table I columns (1)–(7): Δb ≈ −0.015 to −0.018***, Δa ≈ 0.014 to 0.021***, %Δ wages 0.043–0.068***, employment always insignificant.

### DATA DETAILS
- **Number of policy events:** 138 state-level minimum wage changes.
- **Time period:** 1979–2016.
- **Number of observations:** 847,314 (quarter-bin cells) in cols (1)–(6); 14,484 in col (7).
- **Number of workers in the sample:** 4,694,104 (underlying CPS sample used to build job counts).
- **Unit of observation:** quarter × $0.25 wage-bin cells; geographic level = state.
- **Baseline jobs below new MW (b̄₋₁):** 0.086 of total employment; average %ΔMW = 0.101 (10.1%).
- **Wage bins:** $0.25 wide; window spans $4 below to $5 above the new minimum wage.
- Standard errors clustered by state.
