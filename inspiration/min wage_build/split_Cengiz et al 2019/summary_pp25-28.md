# Summary: Cengiz, Dube, Lindner & Zipperer (2019), pp. 1429–1432

## What these pages argue
These pages complete the robustness table (Table I continued) and open the heterogeneity analysis (Section III.A). The robustness message: the near-zero employment effect and significant wage effect survive an aggressive battery of controls (state-specific trends, division-period effects) and an alternative "simpler" state-panel estimator. The heterogeneity message: even for demographic groups where the minimum wage bites much harder (dropouts, teens), missing jobs are matched closely by excess jobs — so there is little evidence of labor-labor substitution at the bottom.

## Key concepts / equations
- **%ΔW** (affected wage) from equation (2); **% affected employment** = (Δa+Δb)/b̄₋₁; **MW elasticity** = (Δa+Δb)/%ΔMW; **own-wage elasticity** = (1/%ΔW)·(Δa+Δb)/b̄₋₁.
- Column (7) uses an **absolute wage threshold W̄ = $15**: regress per-capita wage bill or employment under $15/hr on a state-by-quarter panel — shows results are not driven by wage-bin choices, but cannot separate excess vs. missing jobs.
- Card & Krueger (1995) style **linear probability model** predicts exposure (wage < 125% of statutory minimum) to sort workers into high/middle/low-probability groups.

## Main results / numbers
- Wage effect: 5.7–6.9% (common bin-period FE), 4.3–5.0% (division-specific). Employment: −1.9% to 3.6%, never significant.
- Own-wage elasticity −0.449 (s.e. 0.574) in col (5); collapses to −0.003 (s.e. 0.455) adding quadratic trends (col 6).
- Col (7) panel: affected employment 0.025 (s.e. 0.029), wage 0.063 (s.e. 0.011) — nearly identical to col (1).
- Demographic bite (Δb): dropouts −6.5%, HS-or-less −3.2%, teens −11.4%, women −2.3%, black/Hispanic −2.8% (261% and 78% larger than baseline −1.8% for first two).
- Own-wage elasticities −0.086 to 0.570; only dropouts significant (0.475, s.e. 0.268). Teen MW elasticity 0.125 (s.e. 0.134), insignificant.

### DATA DETAILS
- **Data source:** QCEW-benchmarked CPS; robustness also uses **raw CPS** (Online Appendix Table A.4). Underlying survey = Current Population Survey (CPS).
- **Events:** 138 state-level MW changes, 1979–2016.
- **Table II observations:** 847,314 (846,729 for black/Hispanic col 5) quarter-bin cells; number of events 138 across all columns.
- **Workers per demographic sample:** dropouts 660,771; HS-or-less 2,248,711; teens 287,484; women 2,277,624; black/Hispanic 781,003; high-prob 469,226; medium-prob 1,830,393; low-prob 2,349,485.
- **Exposure model:** estimated on observations 3 years before each of the 138 events and outside all 5-year post-windows; outcome = wage < 125% of statutory minimum.
- Regressions weighted by state-quarter aggregated population (of the demographic group); SEs clustered by state.
- **Robustness restrictions (Appendix A.4):** no-tip-credit states; drop tipping occupations; FTE job counts; hourly workers only; add federal MW changes; raw CPS vs QCEW-benchmarked CPS; no population weights; post-1992 only.
- Alternative event windows (Table A.6) and alternative W̄ upper endpoints (Table A.5).
