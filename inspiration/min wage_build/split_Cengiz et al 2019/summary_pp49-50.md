# Cengiz, Dube, Lindner & Zipperer (2019), pp. 49–50 (journal pp. 1453–1454)

## What these pages contain
These two pages are the **References** (bibliography) of the article — no argument, results, figures, or tables. They list the works cited across the paper's minimum wage, wage-distribution, bunching, and econometric-methods literatures.

## Notable entries (relevant to the paper's method and data)
- **Card (1992); Card & Krueger (1994, 1995)** — foundational minimum wage employment studies and *Myth and Measurement*.
- **Cengiz, Dube, Lindner & Zipperer (2019), "Replication Data for: 'The Effect of Minimum Wages on Low-Wage Jobs'," Harvard Dataverse, doi:10.7910/DVN/TJCTC7** — the paper's own replication archive.
- **DiNardo, Fortin & Lemieux (1996); Lee (1999); Autor, Manning & Smith (2016)** — density/distributional approaches the authors contrast with their frequency-based method.
- **Meer & West (2016)** — the TWFE-logMW dynamic specification the paper critiques.
- **Kleven (2016) "Bunching"; Saez (2010)** — bunching/optimization-friction methods underlying the missing/excess jobs framework.
- **Ferman & Pinto (forthcoming)** — inference with few treated groups and heteroskedasticity (used for event-specific CIs).
- **Neumark, Salas & Wascher (2014); Allegretto et al. (2017)** — the specification-sensitivity debate the paper reconciles.
- **Vaghul & Zipperer (2016), "Historical State and Sub-State Minimum Wage Data," Washington Center for Equitable Growth Working Paper** — the source of the minimum wage policy variable used in the paper.

### DATA DETAILS
- **Replication data & code:** Cengiz et al. (2019), **Harvard Dataverse, doi:10.7910/DVN/TJCTC7**.
- **Minimum wage series source:** **Vaghul & Zipperer (2016), "Historical State and Sub-State Minimum Wage Data," Washington Center for Equitable Growth Working Paper, 2016** — the historical state/sub-state minimum wage dataset used to code the 138 events.
